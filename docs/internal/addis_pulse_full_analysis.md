## Part 1 — What AddisPulse Media Is: Full Business Understanding

### 1.1 The One-Line Definition

AddisPulse Media is a **software-enabled, in-vehicle advertising and lead-generation platform** that turns Addis Ababa's ride-share ecosystem into a measurable customer acquisition channel for premium advertisers.

It is not:
- A taxi sticker business ("out of home" advertising)
- A digital marketing agency
- A generic QR code tool

It **is** the intersection of three things that have never been combined in Ethiopia before:
1. **Physical media** — a captive, premium audience inside a moving vehicle
2. **Digital measurement** — QR → landing page → form → OTP → verified lead → dashboard
3. **Field operations discipline** — trained, compliant, incentivised driver network as a supply chain

---

### 1.2 The Market Problem Being Solved

**The problem is measurement, not reach.**

Addis Ababa's advertisers — real estate developers, banks, private hospitals, universities — have money to spend. They already use billboards, radio, print, influencers, and Facebook. The problem is that none of these channels tell you *who* responded, *whether* they are a real buyer, or *what* it cost to acquire one qualified lead.

AddisPulse's promise is not "we will show your ad to more people." It is:

> *"We will bring you a list of verified, consented, interested people — with their name, phone number, and intent — and we will show you exactly what it cost per person."*

This is a category-creating offer in the Ethiopian market. There is no competitor currently doing measurable, consent-based lead generation through physical mobility channels in Addis Ababa.

**Why the timing is right (mid-2026 data):**
- **93.2M** mobile connections in Ethiopia (DataReportal, Nov 2025)
- **49M** mobile data/internet users (Ethio Telecom H1 FY2025/26)
- Only **9.8M** social media identities — meaning the typical digital ad strategy misses ~80% of mobile users
- QR scanning is now mainstream; smartphones are the norm in the target demographic

The insight: a hybrid offline-to-digital model captures people who are *on their phones* but *not consistently on social media*. That gap is enormous in Ethiopia.

### 1.3 The Full Product Experience (Every Actor's Journey)

#### The Passenger
```
1. Enters a clean, well-presented ride-share vehicle
2. Driver gives a brief, non-pushy welcome: "There's a QR code in the back with an offer
   — completely optional to scan"
3. Passenger sees: a premium card in the seat organizer, a small refreshment (water/treat),
   and a clear branded offer (e.g., "Get a free property brochure + callback from [Developer]")
4. Passenger scans the QR with their phone camera — no app needed
5. A fast mobile landing page loads: company branding, short video/image, offer description,
   and a short form (name + phone, optionally email and interest type)
6. Passenger submits → receives an OTP SMS → verifies → sees a thank-you screen
7. Within minutes: receives the promised follow-up (brochure link, coupon code, or callback
   confirmation) via SMS or WhatsApp
```

The passenger experience is **hospitality-first, never aggressive**. The reward (refreshment + digital benefit) is the conversion mechanism. Passengers have time, privacy, and a reason to engage.

#### The Driver
```
1. Receives a campaign kit: QR card for seat organizer, inventory (water/treats), driver
   script card, ID/verification sticker
2. Every morning before starting: sends a setup photo (vehicle interior, materials visible)
   via Telegram bot command (/checkin)
3. During the day: introduces the campaign with the scripted welcome — does NOT pressure,
   does NOT manually collect numbers
4. Every 3 days: receives inventory restock; vehicle is inspected for cleanliness
5. At end of week: sees their compliance score and lead count on the leaderboard
6. At campaign close: receives base fee + verified-lead bonus + any top-driver prize,
   MINUS any compliance deductions for dirty setup, missing photos, or suspected fraud
```

The driver is **not** a passive billboard host. The driver is an active participant in lead quality, and their incentives are structured accordingly.

#### The Advertiser (Client)
```
1. Signs a campaign agreement with AddisPulse; pays 70%+ upfront
2. Campaign is set up: landing page configured with their branding, offer, and form fields;
   QR codes generated and printed
3. During the campaign: receives weekly performance updates; can access live dashboard
   showing total scans, verified leads, driver performance, daily trend, cost per lead
4. After campaign ends: receives a professional 10-section final report (reach estimate,
   digital engagement, lead quality, driver performance, zone insights, cost analysis,
   sales follow-up status, recommendations)
5. Uses the verified lead export (CSV) to fuel their sales team's follow-up calls
```

#### The AddisPulse Operations Team
```
1. Manages driver recruitment, vetting, training, and daily compliance
2. Monitors the admin dashboard for fraud signals, low-performing drivers, and anomalies
3. Manages campaign configuration, QR generation, and inventory logistics
4. Delivers client reports and handles account communication
5. Runs random lead audit calls to validate lead quality
```

### 1.5 Why This Business Is Defensible (The Moat)

1. **Driver network quality** — takes time to recruit, vet, train, and discipline a reliable pool. Hard to replicate quickly.
2. **Software stack** — campaign management, fraud detection, OTP verification, lead verification, client dashboard. Proprietary to Bluecore.
3. **Campaign data** — after 3–5 campaigns, AddisPulse knows which zones convert best, which offer types get highest scan rates, and what CPL benchmarks look like by sector. Data becomes a pricing and pitch advantage.
4. **Advertiser relationships** — a successfully-executed campaign with a case study is the strongest barrier to a copycat.

The long-term vision is a **media network**: owning the infrastructure (dashboard, driver network, campaign data, advertiser relationships) that allows the business to scale across sectors, vehicle types, and cities.

## Part 2 — SRS Gap Analysis (Corrected and Evidence-Grounded)

### How to Read This Section

Each gap is grounded with:
- **Proposal citation** — exact text from the business proposal that creates the requirement
- **SRS citation** — the specific clause (or absence) in the SRS that fails to address it
- **Verdict** — confirmed gap, partial gap, or withdrawn

---

### GAP-01: Driver-Level QR Attribution Is Absent 🔴 BLOCKING

**Proposal says (Section 6, Technology Platform):**
> "Each vehicle/driver receives a unique QR link so scans and leads can be tied to a specific driver, location group, and campaign."
> "Disable or penalize suspicious driver QR codes."
> "Driver ranking: Leads and compliance score by driver." (Section 17)

**SRS has:**
FR-QR-01 to FR-QR-07 — one QR per *company profile*. URL encodes `/c/[company_identifier]`. No driver entity. No `driver_id` on Lead record. No driver-level aggregate metrics.

**Why blocking:**
Without driver-level QR attribution you cannot calculate driver bonuses, build the leaderboard, detect per-driver fraud, generate the driver performance section of the campaign report, or disable a specific driver's QR.

**Required:**
- `Driver` entity: `driver_id`, `name`, `vehicle_id`, `zone`, `telegram_handle`, `status`
- `DriverCampaignAssignment`: `driver_id` + `campaign_id` → unique `qr_token`
- QR URL: `/c/[campaign_id]/[driver_token]`
- `Lead.driver_id` foreign key
- Driver aggregates: `scan_count`, `lead_count`, `verified_lead_count`, `compliance_score`

---

### GAP-02: No Campaign Entity Exists 🔴 BLOCKING

**Proposal says:**
> "15-day proof-of-concept using 20 vetted ride-share vehicles." (Section 8)
> "Campaign overview: client, objective, dates, vehicle count, zones." (Section 17, Report)
> "30 days / 40–60 cars / stronger lead target / weekly reporting" — Growth Campaign (Section 9)

**SRS has:**
Static `Company Profile` — no start/end date, no vehicle count, no campaign type, no budget, no KPIs, no status lifecycle. The system treats everything as a permanent profile that exists indefinitely.

**Why blocking:**
The entire business model is time-bounded campaigns. A "15-day real estate campaign with 20 vehicles" cannot be expressed in the current SRS data model.

**Required:**
- `Campaign` entity: `campaign_id`, `advertiser_id`, `campaign_type`, `start_date`, `end_date`, `vehicle_count`, `target_zones[]`, `target_leads`, `budget_ETB`, `status (draft|active|paused|completed)`
- `Advertiser` entity: `advertiser_id`, `company_name`, `sector`, `contact_name`, `billing_info`

---

### GAP-03: OTP / Phone Verification Is Missing 🔴 BLOCKING

**Proposal says (Section 6, Lead Verification):**
> "OTP, duplicate detection, invalid number filtering, consent tracking, and time-stamped lead records."
> "OTP or call-back verification for qualified leads." (Section 6, Fraud Control)
> "Verified leads: Leads passing phone/duplicate/consent checks — Main client value." (Section 17)

**SRS has:**
FR-LCF-03: client-side phone format validation. FR-LCF-07: blocks same IP+phone after 3 submissions in 24h. The word "OTP" does not appear in the SRS.

**Why blocking:**
The "verified lead" is the core commercial unit. A form submission that passes basic phone format validation is not a verified lead. Advertisers in real estate and finance will quickly discover the difference.

**Required:**
- Post-submit OTP flow: generate 6-digit code → SMS via Africa's Talking → passenger enters code → lead status = `otp_verified`
- `Lead.verification_status`: `submitted | otp_sent | otp_verified | otp_failed | call_verified | rejected`
- Duplicate detection: same phone → `rejected` within same campaign
- Invalid number detection: non-Ethiopian format → auto-reject
- SMS API integration is therefore a v1 requirement, not optional

---

### GAP-04: Fraud Detection Logic Is Absent 🔴 BLOCKING

**Proposal says (Section 6, Fraud Control):**
> "One phone number counted once per campaign."
> "Flag repeated submissions from the same device or driver."
> "Compare driver scans with daily passenger and inventory estimates."
> "Disable or penalize suspicious driver QR codes."

**SRS has:**
FR-LCF-07: 3 submissions per 24h per IP+phone. FR-LCF-08: CAPTCHA if >10 submissions/hour per IP. These are generic spam controls, not driver-fraud detection.

**Why blocking:**
A dishonest driver can ask friends/family to scan their specific QR and submit forms with real phone numbers. The IP-based rate limiter does not catch this because submissions come from different devices (different passengers' phones).

**Required:**
- Per-driver scan velocity monitoring: if `driver_X` generates >N leads/day vs. campaign baseline → alert admin
- Device fingerprint flag: same fingerprint submitting to same driver's QR multiple times → flag
- `Lead.fraud_flag` (boolean) + `Lead.fraud_reason` (text)
- Admin panel: fraud alert feed, disable specific driver's QR token
- Inventory cross-check: if driver reports 12 passengers/day but has 40 scans → anomaly alert
- `FraudAuditLog`: admin audit call outcomes, lead invalidation records

---

### GAP-05: Advertiser Dashboard Not Specified as Separate Role 🟠 HIGH

**Proposal says:**
> "Live report showing scans, leads, cost per lead, driver performance, campaign days, exportable lead list, and final summary." (Section 6, Client Dashboard)
> "Conversion status: Client sales team update after follow-up." (Section 17)

**SRS has:**
Section 5.2 defines a `Company Representative` role. But this maps poorly — the SRS's "Company Rep" is a company that has a QR profile, not an advertiser who has paid for a time-bounded campaign. No campaign-scoped dashboard. No CPL KPI. No driver performance view. No PDF report generation.

**Required:**
- `Advertiser` role (distinct from Admin and Company Rep)
- Campaign-scoped dashboard: total scans, unique devices, verified leads, CPL, scan-to-lead rate, daily trend, driver leaderboard
- Lead status pipeline: `new | contacted | appointment_set | converted | rejected` — advertiser can update
- PDF report: the 10-section campaign report template (Section 17 of proposal)

---

### GAP-06: Driver Operations Panel Not Specified 🟠 HIGH

**Proposal says:**
> "Driver operations panel — Telegram MVP first, then mobile app: daily photos, inventory status, leaderboard, warning notices, and bonus tracking." (Section 6)
> "Daily: Driver sends setup photo before starting campaign rides." (Section 7)

**SRS has:**
No `Driver` role. No Telegram integration. No photo upload. No inventory tracking. No bonus/compliance reporting.

**Required (MVP via Telegram Bot):**
- `/checkin [photo]` — driver uploads setup photo; stored in Supabase Storage
- `/inventory [count]` — driver reports remaining inventory
- `/score` — bot replies with lead count, compliance score, bonus estimate
- Admin panel: view all driver check-ins, flag missing check-ins
- `DriverComplianceRecord`: `driver_id`, `campaign_id`, `date`, `checkin_photo_url`, `inventory_reported`, `compliance_flag`

---

### GAP-07: Passenger Reward / Inventory Tracking Absent 🟠 HIGH

**Proposal says:**
> "Reward every campaign: Water, treat, coupon, discount." (Section 4)
> "Passenger inventory: 3,600 passengers × 15 ETB water/treat average = 54,000 ETB" (Section 8, Pilot Hard Cost)
> "Inventory leakage — Three-day restock cycle, scan-to-stock reconciliation." (Section 13, Risk Control)

**SRS has:**
No mention of rewards, inventory, or restock cycles.

**Required:**
- `Reward` entity linked to `Campaign`: `reward_type`, `quantity_per_vehicle_per_day`, `unit_cost_ETB`
- `InventoryRecord`: `driver_id`, `campaign_id`, `date`, `issued_count`, `reported_remaining`, `discrepancy_flag`
- Restock alert: trigger AddisPulse ops team every 3 days per driver
- Inventory vs. scan ratio anomaly detection

---

### GAP-08: Lead Status Pipeline Mis-categorised as Future Feature 🟠 HIGH

**Proposal says:**
> "Lead status: new, contacted, appointment set, converted, rejected." (Section 6, Minimum Dashboard Metrics)
> "Conversion status: Client sales team update after follow-up — Shows business outcome beyond marketing." (Section 17, required dashboard field)

**SRS has:**
FR-RLD-02 mentions `submission_status (new, viewed, followed-up, etc.)` as a stored field — good.
BUT Section 14 (Optional Future Features) lists "Lead status tracking and sales pipeline view" — contradicting FR-RLD-02 and the proposal.

**Required for v1 (not future):**
- Lead status: `new | contacted | appointment_set | converted | rejected | duplicate | invalid`
- Advertiser can update status from their dashboard
- Conversion rate = `converted / verified_leads` per campaign
- Final report includes conversion status breakdown

---

### GAP-09: Performance Benchmark — WITHDRAWN ✅ NOT A GAP

**Original claim:** SRS's 10 Mbps 4G benchmark is too optimistic for Ethiopia.

**Correction (with evidence):**
- Ethio Telecom average mobile download: **12.9 Mbps** (SpeedGeo, early 2026)
- Ethio Telecom internal 4G MBB targets: **6.9–8.78 Mbps**
- AddisPulse zones (Bole, Kazanchis, Megenagna) have the **highest 4G infrastructure concentration** in Ethiopia

`NFR-PERF-01` ("under 3 seconds on 4G mobile, 10 Mbps download") is a reasonable, appropriately conservative benchmark for these specific premium urban zones. The SRS is correct here.

**No action required.**

### GAP-09r: Multi-Tenancy Architecture Not Clarified 🟡 MEDIUM (Decision Required)

**Context:**
The SRS supports up to 500 "company profiles." But it doesn't distinguish between:
- (a) Internal tool — Bluecore runs campaigns for clients (single operator)
- (b) Self-service SaaS — advertisers log in and manage their own campaigns
- (c) White-label platform — licensed to other adtech companies in other cities

The proposal says: *"One operator: Bluecore Software PLC at launch"* → strongly implies (a).
The investor thesis implies (b)/(c) over time.

**Required decision:** Confirm MVP = (a). Document (b) as Phase 2. Do not over-engineer for (c) in v1.

## Part 3 — Blocking Questions (Pending Your Answers)

**Q1 — QR per-driver-per-campaign or per-driver-permanent?**
Recommendation: Per-campaign (new QR each campaign). Better fraud isolation and campaign-scoped analytics outweigh the cost of reprinting cards.

**Q2 — Which SMS/OTP provider?**
Options: (a) Africa's Talking, (b) Ethio Telecom bulk SMS (requires corporate agreement), (c) Twilio.
Recommendation: Africa's Talking for MVP speed.

**Q3 — Landing page: Amharic+English or English-only?**
Recommendation: Bilingual from day 1. Amharic form labels and CTAs will measurably increase conversion rates.

**Q4 — Does the advertiser get a live dashboard login during campaign?**
Recommendation: Yes. It is the key differentiator and achievable in MVP.

**Q5 — Is the PDF campaign report auto-generated or manually prepared?**
Recommendation: Manual for pilot (team curates quality). Auto-generate in Phase 2.

**Q6 — Is payment/billing in scope for v1?**
Recommendation: Track invoice status (Paid/Unpaid/Partial) internally. No payment gateway for v1.

**Q7 — Multi-tenancy: single-operator MVP or must v1 support multiple independent operators?**
Recommendation: Single-operator for v1. Clean data model allows multi-tenancy in v2 without schema migration.

**Q8 — Data residency: must personal data be stored in Ethiopia?**
Recommendation: Get legal opinion before production launch. Proceed with Supabase for pilot.

**Q9 — Does Bluecore have existing infrastructure to reuse?**
Domain, SMS API accounts, cloud hosting, developer environment?

**Q10 — When does Telegram bot graduate to native mobile app?**
Recommendation: After Gate 4 (post-3 paid campaigns) + external investment confirmed.
